3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102, without question. One agent with check_rent_due, get_cash_position, request_payment_approval and update_ticket_status needs about 5 tool calls. The team used 10, including get_ticket 3×, check_rent_due 2× and get_cash_position 2×, plus three model contexts at 28.9k tokens. Rent is one lease, one amount, one date. There is nothing to delegate.

Ticket 101, mostly. One agent holding inventory, accounting and draft tools would need about 7 calls instead of 16. It would not necessarily have avoided the $8 duplicate PO, though. Inventory had the invoice description ("Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S") in its own tool result and ordered anyway. That was a reasoning miss, not a hand-off problem, so a single agent could make it too.

The 103 re-run is the clearest case for one context. The refusal ("max 6 units") landed in inventory's context. Inventory handed accounting a summary, accounting misread the stock, and the boss then asked for "another" PO without the full history. That's three partial views of one decision. A single agent that just got refused is far more likely to stop, or to ask the human, than to split the order.

Why we'd still keep a team for some work: least privilege is real. Customer service cannot request money, facilities cannot buy stock. And 103's main run showed a genuinely useful split: count, price, write. But notice where the safety actually came from: the MCP tools (refusals, duplicates, cost floor, no negative balance) and the human signature, not the number of agents. A single agent behind the same 15 tools would be just as safe. Our honest recommendation: a router + one generalist for routine tickets like rent, and specialists only when a ticket crosses domains (stock + price + a customer letter).

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would take

  1. Getting paid. The club accepts the $49.30 price and pays $394.40 for 8 hoodies.
    • Our shop literally can't record it. The rule is "cash only goes out", there is no sales table, and inventory wouldn't drop from 8.
    • Meanwhile the Cash tab shows the shop at $20 with $2,400 rent due 10/02. Revenue is the only way out.
    Needs:
    • A sales table, and a record_sale(ticket, sku, size, qty, unit_price) tool that decrements stock and writes a pending receipt.
    • A human-confirmed confirm_payment_received that raises cash_accounts.
    • A forecast_cash(days) tool that reads leases.next_due and invoice due dates. It would have warned, before anyone signed the $132 of POs, that October rent was uncovered.
    • Agent: a Sales / Treasurer agent (or a widened accounting) that owns money in and the cash forecast.
  2. Receiving stock and actually fulfilling orders. We paid for 1 tee (#2) and 6 hoodies (#5, #6), and the 501 reprint is coming too.
    • Yet inventory still says tee S = 0 and hoodie M = 8. There's no purchase_orders table (POs live only as approval requests), no "arrived" event, and no way to hand Tauhid his shirt or ship the club's box.
    Needs:
    • A purchase_orders table (ordered/shipped/received).
    • receive_shipment(po_id, qty) to update inventory.qty.
    • reserve_stock(ticket_id) / fulfill_order.
    • A human-approved book_courier through QuickShip CT (vendor 3, 1-day lead), which no ticket ever used.
    • Agent: a Receiving & Fulfillment agent that reopens 101 and 103 when the boxes land (around 9/5 for Bulldog's 5-day lead).
  3. Having a conversation with the outside world. Draft 1 asks Tauhid "M, L, XL, or wait?" and draft 2 asks the club "8 now or wait?".
    • The answers can never come back: no inbox, no send, no reply threading.
    • Draft 2 is already stale. It was written before the 6-hoodie POs, and there's no tool to revise it.
    • "Approving a PO" also isn't the same as telling Bulldog to print.
    Needs:
    • send_draft(draft_id), human-approved (the shop rule stays: nothing goes out unsigned).
    • update_draft.
    • ingest_inbox, which turns emails into new tickets or replies on existing ones.
    • submit_vendor_order(po_id).
    • Agent: a Correspondence agent that triages incoming mail to the boss, plus a rule that customer service must refresh a draft whenever the board changes under it.

What I'd change first

  • Enforce what an open invoice is for. check_vendor_can_ship already returns the description; request_purchase_order_approval should refuse a PO for a SKU and size that an open or just-paid invoice already covers. That fixes 101's $8.
  • Refuse PO-splitting. After a request_purchase_order_approval refusal on a ticket and SKU, refuse further POs for that pair unless a human asks. That fixes 103's re-run.
  • Reserve rent. Make get_cash_position's "available" subtract rent due within 30 days, so a hoodie order can't eat October's lease.
